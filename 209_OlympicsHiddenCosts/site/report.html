Olympics — The Hidden Costs

By Floriane Leynaud, Hope Tadsen, Bernard Julve — DATASCI 209, Summer 2026

⌄

Every four years, a new city takes on the Olympic bill. Since 1976, fourteen cities across five continents have played host — and each one has walked away with a very different fiscal and social footprint. In this report, we explore the hidden costs of hosting the Olympics, examining the financial and social impacts on host cities and their residents.

More specifically, we explore:

  1. The debt burden for cities
  2. The cost vs revenue generated by the organizing committee
  3. The GDP outcome in host countries
  4. The CO₂ emissions and impact on happiness

1 — Host cities carry the debt for decades

The Olympic bill doesn't disappear when the closing ceremony ends. Every host since 1976 is plotted below — each circle sized by its sports-related cost (venues and operations, in 2022 US$) and coloured by how long the host carried the debt. Montreal (1976) needed 30 years to pay off its stadium; Athens' (2004) venues still sit on the public books. Use the filter to compare Summer and Winter Games.

🖱️ Hover over any city for its cost, overrun and debt outcome.

* The circle's Cost is the sports-related figure (venues and operations); the all-in Games bill — transport, security and urban infrastructure — is considerably larger. We use it because it is defined consistently across every Games (Flyvbjerg et al., Oxford), so hosts decades apart are directly comparable; broader "total cost" numbers are reported inconsistently and often unavailable.

4 — Beyond the balance sheet and economic indicators

We also want to explore the impacts environmentally and socially that hosting the Olympics may have on the host country.
To explore environmental impacts we will review data from the Emissions Database for Global Atmospheric Research, or “EDGAR”. This data is a multipurpose, independent, global database of anthropogenic emissions of greenhouse gases and air pollution on Earth. Now we will look at social and health indicators

From GDP to Generosity this data set lets us explore how the Olympics impacted happiness accross countries that hosted and those who did not host. To visualized this we chose to use the World Happiness Report ranking, based on self-reported wellbeing.

A happiness score is made up of the following scores:

  • Freedom
  • Life Expectancy
  • Generosity
  • GDP Per Capita
  • Corruption
  • Social Impact

Takeaways

  1. Hosting the Olympics saddles host communities with enormous costs.
  2. Those costs are routinely underestimated — overruns run into the billions.
  3. And it is the public, not the IOC (International Olympic Committee), that carries the debt, often for decades.
